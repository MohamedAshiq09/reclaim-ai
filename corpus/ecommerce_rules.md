# Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020

- **Authority**: Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution, Government of India
- **Official Source URL**: https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/acts-and-rules/consumer-protection/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules-2020
- **Verified Date**: 2026-10-24
- **Scope**: All e-commerce transactions, marketplaces, inventory e-commerce entities, and digital platforms in India.

## Key Provisions & Statutory Rights

### 1. Mandatory Grievance Redressal Officer (Rule 4 & Rule 5)
Every e-commerce entity shall display prominently on its platform:
- The name, designation, and contact details (including phone number and email address) of the Grievance Officer.
- The mechanism by which the consumer may register a complaint.

### 2. Statutory Timelines (Rule 5(4))
- **Acknowledgment**: The Grievance Officer must acknowledge the receipt of any consumer complaint within **forty-eight (48) hours**.
- **Redressal**: The entity must redress the complaint within **one (1) month (30 days)** from the date of receipt of the complaint.

### 3. Return, Refund, and Exchange (Rule 6 & Rule 7)
- No e-commerce entity shall impose cancellation charges on consumers unless similar charges are borne by the entity if they cancel the purchase.
- Entities must effectuate refunds within a reasonable period, typically not exceeding 14 calendar days, once a return or deficiency is verified.
- Delivery of defective, expired, or damaged goods constitutes deficiency of service under Section 2(11) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.

### 4. Next Tier of Escalation
If the entity fails to resolve the grievance within 30 days or ignores the 48-hour acknowledgment:
- National Consumer Helpline (NCH): Toll-Free 1915 or online at `https://consumerhelpline.gov.in`.
- Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission via e-Daakhil (`https://edaakhil.nic.in`).
